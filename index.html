About Michigan

The State of Michigan gets its name from the Native American word Michigama, meaning great or large lake. Michigan, nicked named the Wolverine State, entered statehood on January 26, 1837 as the 26th state of the United States (State of Michigan, n.d.).

Did you know?

Famous inventor Thomas Edison spent much of his childhood in Port Huron, MI. His family moved there from Milan, Ohio in 1854 in the hopes of making a better living (Archives of Michigan., 2026).

Prior to becoming a state Michigan had a breif skirmish with Ohio over the ownership of Toledo. This war was known as the Toledo War and delayed Michigan’s statehood. After Michigan forcefully occupied Toledo President Andrew Jackson offered the Upper Peninsula as a trade for Toledo to be a part of Ohio in order Michigan to becom a state. Michigan accepted the trade and became the only state in the nation comprised of two peninsulas (Michigan DNR, 2021).
